01 · The campus

A building that eats a city's worth of power

Three halls, one industrial yard

This campus runs 144 MW of computers: three halls of 48 MW each. Everything else you see exists to feed them power and take their heat away.

White space
rooms full of racks
Grey space
electrical and cooling rooms, often as big as the white space
Yard
generators, transformers, tanks, batteries

Power is the bottleneck

Electricity arrives at 230,000 volts from two separate lines. Getting a grid connection this large can take years, so time to power now decides where AI campuses are built.

The batteries beside the substation smooth the power swings that AI training creates.

A generator for every few megawatts

Thirteen 3 MW diesel generators line each hall, one more than needed (N+1). If the grid fails, batteries carry the load for the 10 seconds it takes them to start.

The roof is a radiator

Every watt that goes in comes out as heat. Rows of dry coolers on the roof, giant radiators with fans, push that heat into the outside air. Watch the fans turn.

02 · The building

Lift the roof

A windowless box

96 by 44 metres, 11 metres tall. Generators outside, two fibre entrances at opposite ends, and power that arrives by two routes that never share a trench.

Electrical, mechanical, white space

Switchgear and batteries fill one end. A gallery of pumps and coolant distribution units runs along the back wall. The middle is rows of racks, with power and fibre overhead.

Rows in pairs

Racks stand back to back. Their hot exhaust is trapped in a contained aisle and ducted up and away, so it never mixes with the cool air the racks breathe in.

03 · Power

From 230,000 volts to 0.8

Each step down in voltage needs more current to deliver the same power. To carry one megawatt at chip voltage you would need over a million amps, which is why the final conversion happens millimetres from the silicon.

voltage (log scale)current needed to deliver 1 MW

Two paths, never shared

Path A and path B each carry 100% of the load: separate transformers, switchgear, UPS and busway. They meet only at the rack, which has two power cords. That is "2N" redundancy.

04 · Cooling

Warm water, on the chip

Stand in the cold aisle

Racks breathe room air in through their fronts (blue arrows) and blow it out the back into a sealed hot aisle. The person is to scale: each rack is 2.3 m tall.

Pipes to every rack

Above the hot aisle run two headers: blue supply and red return. A branch drops into every rack and feeds cold plates bolted onto each GPU. About 85% of the heat leaves in this water.

The CDU keeps two waters apart

The coolant distribution unit at the end of each row is a heat exchanger with pumps. Clean rack water on one side, facility water on the other, so a leak in one can never drain the other.

05 · The rack

72 GPUs that act as one

An NVL72-class rack

18 compute trays hold 72 GPUs and 36 CPUs. Nine switch trays in the middle join every GPU to every other at 1.8 TB/s each. It draws about 130 kW and weighs about 1.4 tonnes.

Pull out a tray

Each tray holds two superchips: four GPUs and two CPUs. Copper cold plates sit on every chip, with blue and red tubes running back to quick-disconnect couplings.

The back of the rack

Water manifolds run up one side and a copper power busbar up the middle. The NVLink spine, about 5,000 copper cables, is what turns 72 GPUs into one machine.

06 · The network

Racks become a supercomputer

The scalable unit

Eight NVL72 racks, 576 GPUs and about 1 MW of IT load, installed and tested as one block. A cluster is this row, repeated: 16 of them is about 9,000 GPUs.

Light between racks

Copper links GPUs inside a rack. Between racks it is fibre, at 400–800 Gb/s per GPU, running overhead to a row of spine switches. Watch the pulses.

Try it

Why "rail-optimized" networks exist

During training, GPU 0 in every server mostly talks to GPU 0 in the others. Tap any GPU to see where its traffic goes, then switch layouts.

07 · Why AI is different

Ten times the power in the same footprint

Cloud racks roughly tripled in eight years. AI racks went up about tenfold and straight through the limit of air cooling. And because thousands of GPUs compute and pause together, the whole building's power draw can swing by tens of megawatts in under a second.

10 · What comes next

Five things to watch

Bring your own power

On-site gas, giant batteries, restarted nuclear plants, and small reactors in the 2030s.

Megawatt racks

Racks heading toward 1 MW, fed at 800 V DC to cut copper and losses.

Light, not copper

Co-packaged optics put lasers beside the switch chip and stretch GPU domains past one rack.

Mirrors in the network

Optical circuit switches redirect light instead of processing packets.

Flexible demand

Sites that can slow down when the grid is stressed get connected sooner.
