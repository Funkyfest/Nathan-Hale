# Prompts to paste into LM Studio

Attach `data-center-design-knowledge.md` and set `system-prompt.txt` as the system prompt first.

## Warm-up (after one listen of the podcast)

- Quiz me on Part I and Part II. Ten questions, one at a time, keep score.
- Explain why "time to power" replaced fibre as the first site-selection question, like I'm new.
- What are the four kinds of space in a data center, and roughly how big is the grey space compared with the white space?

## The power chain

- Walk me down the voltage ladder from the transmission line to the chip. At each step, tell me the voltage and why it is stepped down there.
- Quiz me on the difference between N, N+1, 2N and 2(N+1). Give me a scenario and ask which one it is.
- Why might an AI training hall deliberately use less redundancy than a Tier III cloud hall? Then ask me to argue the opposite side.
- Drill the numbers: battery ride-through, generator start time, generator size, losses between the grid and the transistor.

## Cooling

- Explain, like I'm new, why water at 35 °C can cool a chip.
- Quiz me on the three loops (facility, technology, air) and what the CDU does between them.
- I say "PUE 1.2". You tell me what that means in watts for a 100 MW hall, then ask me what WUE and CUE measure.
- Mock interview: the interviewer wants to know whether I would choose dry coolers or evaporative cooling towers for a site in Arizona, and why.

## White space and network

- What is the difference between scale-up and scale-out? Use the NVL72 rack as the example.
- Explain rail-optimized networking in three sentences, then quiz me with a diagram described in words.
- Why are optical transceivers such a large share of network cost, and what is co-packaged optics?

## The AI factory

- List the five properties that make an AI data center different from a cloud data center, then quiz me on each.
- Explain the power-swing problem to a utility engineer, then to my grandmother.
- Summarize Part VII in five bullets.

## Operations, money, future

- Quiz me on the five commissioning levels.
- Drill the numbers from the worked example: racks, GPUs, electricity, coolant flow, cost, weight for a 100 MW hall.
- Give me the five "what comes next" trends and ask me to rank them by how soon they will matter. Push back on my ranking using the guide.

## Spaced repetition

- Pick twelve facts from across the whole guide that I am most likely to forget. Ask them to me one at a time. For each one I miss, tell me which part to re-read.
- Pretend it is a week from now. Give me a 15-question final exam covering all nine parts, grade it, and tell me my weakest part.
