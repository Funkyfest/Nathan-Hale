# Rebalancing rules for Course A: stay fully invested

Decision taken November 4, 2026. Companion to `market-outlook-2026-11.html`. These are
rules for Nathan to review with Drew and Ian before anything is executed. Nothing here is
a trade instruction.

## Why rules, not a cash raise

Course A keeps the highest expected 36-month return (about 22% in the modeled mix) and
accepts a worst 12-month stretch near minus 13% if the bear case arrives. The risk in
Course A is behavioral, not mathematical: selling after a 20% drop. Written rules, set
now while the index is 3% off its record, are what remove that decision from the moment.

## The rules

1. **Rebalance on a calendar and on drift, whichever comes first.** Quarterly on the
   first trading day of January, April, July and October, or any day a sleeve is 5
   percentage points away from target. Targets are the current policy mix, written
   down once with Drew and not changed inside a drawdown.
2. **Concentration caps.** No single stock above 8% of the equity sleeve. No sector
   above 35%. The Magnificent Seven are near a third of the S&P 500 by weight, so a
   plain index fund already sits at the cap; add a value, dividend or equal-weight
   sleeve rather than selling the index.
3. **Trim winners inside tax-advantaged accounts first.** Any trim that the caps
   require happens in the IRA and 401(k) before any taxable lot is touched. In taxable
   accounts, sell only lots held over a year, and pair gains with harvested losses.
4. **Buy the bear case in thirds.** If the S&P 500 closes 15% below its record (about
   6,650), move one third of the next twelve months of planned contributions and any
   maturing cash into equities. Another third at 25% down (about 5,860). The last third
   at 30% down (about 5,470) or on the Fed's first cut, whichever comes first.
5. **Keep the kids' accounts untouched.** Their horizon is decades. Contributions
   continue on schedule and increase, not pause, in a selloff.
6. **Hold enough liquidity to never sell into a drawdown.** Even in Course A, the
   household's next twelve months of known spending sits in a money-market fund or
   T-bills. Treasury interest is state-tax exempt, so that reserve belongs in taxable
   accounts.
7. **Headlines are not triggers.** Election results, merger rumors and forecasts from
   any single voice do not move the plan. Only the tripwires below do, and they move
   scenario weights, not the allocation, unless a cap or drift rule fires.
